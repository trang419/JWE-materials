Introduction

  • Understand the structure of Balance of Payments (BoP)
  • Examine Japan’s external accounts and capital flows
  • Explain the relationship between the current, capital, and financial accounts
  • Interpret Japan’s current account, financial account, and international investment position

What is BOP ?

  • record all cross-border transactions of a country
  • three components (IMF BPM, 6th ed)
    • current account: trade flow, income flow, transfers
    • capital account: non-market transactions flow of assets
    • financial account: capital flow

  • in the definition that existed prior to 2014, what is known today as the financial account was called the capital account
  • Current account:
    • export and import of goods and services,
    • investment income and unilateral transfers such as foreign aid
  • Capital account:
    • transactions in nonproduced, nonfinancial assets, such as land
    • unilateral capital transfers, such as government debt forgiveness
  • Financial account: cross-border transactions of assets (other than nonproduced, nonfinancial assets): capital inflow and outflows
    • acquisition of a business (direct investment)
    • acquisition of financial products for asset management (portfolio investment)

Current Account (Imports of Goods) -100,000

Foreigners visit Japanese and pay for their holiday by using money deposited in their country’s bank accounts.

BoP Account Credit (+ inflow) Debit (− outflow)
Current Account (Export of Services) 100,000
Financial Account (Bank deposits oversea account) -100,000
  • when the purchases of foreign assets exceed the sales of domestic assets to foreigners (with net receipt of foreign assets), the financial account shows a positive number

Relationships between the accounts

  • Current Account + Capital Account: net receive from the rest of the world in return for the exports of goods and other resources

  • Financial Account: net increase foreign assets

  • Current Account + Capital Account = Financial Account - Errors and Omissions

  • BOP is a description of the international budget constraint for the country

  • a country has to have a surplus in current and capital accounts to invest abroad
  • it has to borrow from abroad when it runs deficits in the sum of current and capital accounts

  • The financial account records a country’s flow of international transactions in assets and liabilities over a period, while the net international investment position (NIIP) is a stock measure of its total external assets and liabilities at a specific point in time
  • The financial account’s transactions, such as buying or selling stocks and bonds, directly contribute to changes in the NIIP from one period to the next, along with changes in asset values due to exchange rates and market price movements

Japan’s current account components

dt = fread("../data/09 bop/mof_ca.csv", skip=2)
setnames(dt,colnames(dt)[[1]],"year")
dt = melt(dt,id.vars = "year",variable.name = "item")
dt[,item:=trimws(gsub("/.*$","",item))]
dt[,year:=as.integer(year)]
dt[,type:=ifelse(grepl("Current",item),"Total","Component")]
dt[,value:=value/10^4]

ggplot() +
  geom_col(data = dt[type=="Component"],
           aes(x = year, y = value, fill = item),
           position="stack") +
  geom_line(data = dt[type=="Total"] ,
            aes(x = year, y = value), color="black", size=1) +
  geom_hline(yintercept=0, color="gray40") +
  labs(title="Japan: Current Account Components and Total",
       x=NULL, y="Net balance (tril yen)", 
       fill="Component", caption = "Source: MOF") +
  theme_minimal() +
  theme(legend.position="bottom")
  • Service trade always deficit: important driver is international tourism balance, run large deficits but turned positive in 2015.
  • Net exports of goods replaced by primary income (i.e., interest and dividends from external assets) after 2007
  • Large trade deficit in 2011: natural gas to generate electricity aftermath of earthquake and nuclear plants shutdown
  • Large trade deficit in 2022: high energy import costs due to the war in Ukraine and the depreciation of the yen making imports more expensive

  • A current account surplus implies an increase in external assets (i.e., surpluses of the financial accounts)
  • Composition Dominated by direct investment and portfolio assets. Shift from lending (1980s) → equity ownership (post-2000).
  • Net direct investment has increased steadily
  • Net portfolio investment abroad has been very volatile
    • in 2011: partly due to Japanese insurance companies’ repatriation of assets to pay for insurance reimbursements
  • Official reserve: MOF (BOJ being its agent) occasionally intervened in the foreign exchange market to buy the US dollar (and sell the yen)
    • net external assets increase in 2003–2004 and 2011

Japan’s NIIP

dc = fread("../data/09 bop/oecd_niip.csv")
setnames(dc, tolower(names(dc)))
setnames(dc,"niip","value")
latest = dc[, .SD[which.max(time)], by = country]
outliers <- c(
  latest[order(-value)][1:3, country],
  latest[order(value)][1:3, country],
  "Japan"
)
ggplot(dc, aes(x = time, y = value/10^6, group = country,
               color = country == "Japan", size = country == "Japan")) +
  geom_line(alpha = 0.6) +
  geom_text_repel(
    data = latest[country %in% outliers],
    aes(label = country),
    nudge_x = 0.3, size = 3, color = "black"
  ) +
  scale_color_manual(values = c("gray70", "red")) +
  scale_size_manual(values = c(0.5, 1.3)) +
  labs(
    title = "Net International Investment Position (NIIP)",
    subtitle = "Trillion USD — Japan highlighted in red",
    x = NULL, y = "Trillion USD"
  ) +
  theme_minimal(base_size = 13) +
  theme(legend.position = "none")
  • Level Japan has one of the world’s largest positive NIIPs (~US$3–4 trillion, >80% of GDP 2024). Reflects decades of current account surpluses and outward FDI. (US: -90% of its nominal GDP 2024)
  • Resilience Stable external position despite aging and yen fluctuations. Suggests strong net creditor status and global financial reach.
